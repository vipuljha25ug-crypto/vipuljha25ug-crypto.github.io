Reflection on creating a Chatbot using Typebot

Typebot has allowed me to build a chatbot designed specifically to provide potential students interested in gaining admission to Azim Premji University with information through an interactive conversational style. Prior to undertaking this project, I took time learning about the typebot, experimenting with different blocks of content in order to understand the various types of information that would be useful in creating an effective way to communicate with users about potential admission opportunities and being familiarized with how the interface works and what different features (i.e., text messages, buttons, input fields) could be used to structure the flow of the conversation within the chatbot.There were a number of strategies used when planning how to structure the chatbot. I first came up with a general idea of the kinds of information like courses available, admissions process, scholarships, student life on campus, a prespective student would be interested in learning about and developed a menu of sections that would be included in the chatbot based on that information. I also addded a option where the chatbot is asking the name of the user and send a greeting message after to get a sense of familiarity. I thought that typebot was simple to learn and use overall. I think the drag and drop options to build typebot was very easy to create blocks without using any programming language. Overall I will say that it was easy and fun to build this chatbot

Reflection on the Google Flu Trends

As I have examined the concept of failure and its association with technological innovation and the development of the Failure Wiki project, it has become apparent to me that a singular cause cannot typically be identified for any failures related to the use of technology (for example, a design flaw) nor can one location be assigned as a point of origin for some technological failures (for example, there is no single country from which all technology originates). However, in my opinion, there are multiple factors that may contribute to the reasons why technology fails (for example, miscommunication between designers, users, and manufacturers; an over-reliance on technology by them, an incorrect understanding of how the technology operates. One clear example of this is the case of Google’s Flu Trends. I believe that Flu Trends is an interesting failure because it demonstrates how even a large company such as Google can make mistakes in attempts to use large data sets to make predictions regarding real-world phenomena when the phenomena are complex (for example, influenza). While on the surface it appeared that Google Flu Trends was a highly innovative system that employed large-scale, anonymous user search data to predict the prevalence of influenza more quickly and accurately than traditional public health surveillance systems, upon further examination, I learned that as time progressed, Flu Trends began producing inaccurate numbers due to changes in user search patterns that resulted from extensive media coverage and growing public awareness of the severity of the flu. Therefore, as a result of users searching for influenza-related terms with greater frequency, Google’s algorithm produced increasingly inflated numbers than what actually existed in reality.
